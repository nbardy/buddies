## The idea: the logo is where the song starts

The overload is noise; the buddies turn it into music. The robots' boops are not effects but the
first notes of the song's hook, on its tempo. "Replying…" rolling up is the song's build. The lock
is the **drop**: the first downbeat of the track, with the full band (kick, bass, wide chords, the
hook), and it **keeps playing** under "buddies", the home statement and the benefits. One song from
the reveal to the end card, so the exciting moment is the music arriving, not an effect.

| Time | Picture | Sound (plan) |

| lock | logo locks | **drop, bar 1**: full groove and the hook, no stinger on top |

| benefits | four lines, one per bar | full groove back, one accent per line |
| demos → Vim → end | as now | groove, then the existing coda and end chord re-voiced in the new key |

The picture changes very little: the robots' pop times and the lock move a few frames to land on
the song's beats.

## Quality bar, honestly

The hits sounded cheap partly because they were bare oscillators: no mixing, no stereo, plain
reverb. The new cue uses edm.py's production chain (stereo supersaws, sidechain, a real reverb,
a master limiter, stems), and I'll judge it **in context**, rendering 8–45 s of the cut rather than
an isolated clip. I can't hear, though: the owner's ears are the only real check, so the first
deliverable is one 35 s in-context draft, not three variations.
